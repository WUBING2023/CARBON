# Recurrent processing distinguishes repeated short cores in native binding profiles

An identical short recognition core can occur more than once in the same RNA window, yet the measured signal need not be distributed equally between the two instances. We asked whether CARBON distinguishes their relative support beyond a local model with an explicit position prior. For PTBP1, CTCT instances with centers at positions 5–95 in each original 101-nt window were processed in sequence order; the first two instances passing a greedy center-spacing requirement of at least 12 nt defined separated inclusive 11-position neighborhoods. The endpoint was the squared difference between predicted and observed site-1 fractions conditional on signal in these neighborhoods. Observed fractions were adjusted using the original size-matched input profile; negative residuals were excluded without truncation. The strict cohort comprised 1,851 task-local locus representatives mapping uniquely to 659 real GENCODE genes across K562 and HepG2, including 72 observed ties in the continuous endpoint. This question concerns relative native profile allocation, rather than binding probability, nucleotide attribution accuracy or downstream functional activity. Identical short cores also need not have equal complete-motif affinity.

Removing the recurrent pathway increased gene-equal paired-share error from 0.079884 in Full to 0.115116 in original NoGRU. That comparison also reduced model size from 100,826 to 44,698 parameters, and recurrent processing can encode absolute window position. We addressed both alternatives with a prespecified local-position control. It retained the NoGRU convolutional extractor, global shared SE gate, normalization and output heads, but added a pointwise position-conditioned residual MLP and a learnable 101-position profile bias. The MLP received local features and fixed coordinates t and t², without communicating between positions. Its 100,871 parameters differ from Full by only 45. All six fits used fresh shared initialization, the complete original training and validation data, maximum 100 epochs, patience 9 and validation-only checkpoint selection. The zero-initialized residual output and profile bias reproduced original NoGRU outputs exactly before training and learned nonzero parameters in every fit.

Full retained a substantial advantage over this near-capacity local-position control: gene-equal error was 0.079884 versus 0.112084, a 28.7% relative reduction (paired difference −0.032200; Bonferroni-2 joint-gene bootstrap interval −0.039564 to −0.024750; Fig. 4c). Ranking accuracy was 76.74% versus 66.31%. Each cell and all three training seeds showed the same error direction. The control itself improved on original NoGRU by −0.003032 (Bonferroni-2 interval −0.005049 to −0.001038), demonstrating that it learned useful local-feature and position information. The Full advantage persisted for unadjusted eCLIP shares (5,067 pairs, 988 genes; −0.037844) and after excluding genes/loci recorded in the historical exposure ledger (535 pairs, 260 genes; −0.027153); both conservative Bonferroni-90 intervals remained below zero. LEPR illustrates a corrected ordering and OCRL a worsened ordering (Fig. 4a,b). These native windows were locked as post hoc median illustrations of the original decision-transition groups before the new control predictions. They do not replace the cohort comparison or imply that every footprint is recovered. Earlier hash-selected counterexamples remain in a separate candidate supplementary figure.

Supporting checks constrain the interpretation. Simple center, pyrimidine, PhyloP, GC and input ordering rules achieved approximately 52.4%, 57.7%, 50.6%, 45.3% and 51.7% gene-equal ranking accuracy. Unfit symmetric Gaussian position profiles with fixed widths 15 and 25 nt yielded errors of 0.221614 and 0.150381. In the post hoc distance diagnostic, Full retained an advantage over the trained local-position control among 195 pairs from 150 genes whose core centers differed by at most 5 nt in distance from the window center (−0.027823; Bonferroni-3 interval −0.047997 to −0.007633). Exact equal-distance and at-most-2-nt subsets were smaller and their intervals crossed zero. No native pair had identical complete 17-nt support inputs including PhyloP; this empty stricter cohort was retained without redefining eligibility. Cross-protein support from six complete HNRNPK NoGRU fits comprised 1,418 pairs and 527 genes: Full error was 0.080893 versus 0.106821, with a −0.025929 difference and Bonferroni-36 interval −0.035348 to −0.016202 (Fig. 4c). HNRNPK was not tested against the near-capacity position control, so its comparison does not isolate capacity or position effects.

Together, the PTBP1 control experiment supports an advantage of recurrent position-coupled processing over this near-capacity pointwise local-position model in allocating measured native support between repeated short cores. It does not establish a particular distant sequence rule, biochemical contact mechanism or conservation-specific explanation. The separate Full-versus-Sequence/Mean/Perm conservation analyses remained inconclusive, including the frozen chrX/Y ambiguity test; these outcomes are retained. Chromosomes chr1/X/Y were excluded from matched training and validation, but the study domain had prior analysis exposure. The claim is a reproducible model-heldout allocation advantage under the stated controls, rather than completely blind biological or downstream functional validation.
