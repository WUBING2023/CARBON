## Main Methods paragraph

We further examined natural single-nucleotide variants using the published BEAPR allele-specific RBP-binding calls (Yang et al., 2019). The previously analysed chr8/15 subset contained 396 significant ASB events at 287 GRCh38 REF/ALT variants across 85 RBPs. We reused the original allele-paired predictions and reference PhyloP inputs, compared the predicted direction with the source's normalized allele preference, and retained a constant reference-allele preference rule. Public population and clinical annotations were matched to the exact GRCh38 alleles. Conservation associations were descriptive and adjusted for task, local sequence, base composition, mutation class, strand, sequencing depth and reference preference (Section S1.20; Data S25).

## Main Results paragraph

We next asked whether sequence perturbations corresponded to experimentally observed preferences at natural SNVs, and whether reference conservation was associated with correct allele ordering (Fig. 5d,e). In the chr8/15 subset, Full correctly ordered 52.0% of the significant ASB events after RBP-equal aggregation, compared with 58.8% for Sequence and 64.8% for the constant reference-preference rule. The Full−Sequence difference was −6.73 percentage points, with a two-way RNA-region/RBP multiplier interval of −15.97 to +1.64 points. Full did not improve consistently over its fixed-input flat- or permuted-PhyloP controls. The adjusted center-PhyloP slope for Full's directional credit was 0.044 (approximate 95% interval, −0.074 to +0.161), and its slope for ranked predicted perturbation magnitude was 0.058 (−0.132 to +0.254); full weighted-refit and region-adjusted sensitivities also spanned zero. These analyses do not establish a conservation-dependent improvement in natural-variant interpretation. The subset had already been examined and shared the ENCODE assay family with model development; it therefore provides a bounded descriptive analysis rather than new independent validation.

## S1.20 Natural SNV annotations and descriptive conservation analyses

### Cohort and outcome

We retained all 7,262 previously qualified BEAPR events across 140 RBPs and 200 RBP–cell tasks. Events comprise a variant, RBP and cell context, not independent biological replicates. The cohort was split as previously defined into 5,409 training, 1,457 validation and 396 chr8/15 events; the last group comprises 287 unique loci/REF–ALT variants, 85 RBPs and 119 RNA-region clusters. Both the complete cohort and the chr8/15 subset had been exposed in earlier analyses. The source contains significant ASB calls (source FDR<0.1) and normalized major/minor *allele identities*, but not usable numerical normalized allele-specific binding effects. We therefore evaluated the preferred direction, not a quantitative binding effect, pathogenicity, or ASB detection. Raw replicate counts were retained as depth diagnostics and never used to replace the normalized preferred-allele label. AUROC/AP are inappropriate for this ASB-positive-only table.

Existing whole-window unique/continuous liftover and reciprocal central-position checks were retained. Each 101-nt allele pair differs only at index 50 (zero-based). The genome-plus REF allele must equal one of the two experimentally compared alleles; this held for every qualified event. REF/ALT annotation queries use GRCh38 coordinates and genome-plus alleles; model sequences remain oriented to the RNA strand. Both alleles receive the same reference hg38 PhyloP100way vector. This is reference conservation held fixed during the perturbation, not a variant-specific evolutionary score.

For each existing checkpoint we subtracted the minor-preference count-head output from the major-preference output. Values >10⁻⁷ receive credit 1, values of absolute magnitude ≤10⁻⁷ receive credit 0.5, and negative values receive credit 0. The mean of the three permuted-Φ predictions is formed before calculating its sign. The constant REF rule receives credit 1 when REF is the source's normalized major allele and 0 otherwise. Predictions represent differences in model log-total-count output; they are not calibrated affinity or experimental allele-count ratios. Full and Sequence reuse single existing checkpoints with incompletely matched training histories. Flat Φ and three permuted Φ inputs perturb the same Full checkpoint; they are not retrained ablations.

Direction accuracy was averaged over events within each cell task, tasks within each RBP, and RBPs with equal weights. The primary displayed uncertainty resamples paired RBP summaries 5,000 times (seed 20261012). To retain shared-region dependence, a secondary two-way positive-exponential multiplier analysis assigns weights to RNA-region clusters within tasks, averages tasks equally within RBP, and then applies independent RBP weights (2,000 draws; seed 20261015). A gene-connected-region cluster does not imply that all its SNVs map to one uniquely identified mature RNA. The RBP bootstrap alone does not capture all shared-locus dependence.

### Conservation association

Within each cohort separately, center PhyloP was ranked with average ranks for ties and scaled to mean zero/standard deviation one. For perturbation magnitude, the outcome was the similarly standardized rank of the absolute allele-paired predicted output difference. For correctness, the outcome was the 0/0.5/1 directional credit, so its slope denotes change in credit per standard deviation of ranked Φ, conditional on the stated controls. Nuisance regressors were task indicators; 101-nt and central 7-nt GC fractions; one-hot bases at the five central positions; genome-plus REF>ALT mutation class; RNA strand; log(1+the total of retained raw read counts); and the source's reference-allele preference. Some categorical effects are redundant with task indicators; the pseudoinverse preserves the common fitted subspace. Local sequence encoding is a limited control, not an independently measured cognate-motif score.

The primary slope interval uses a first-order two-way positive-exponential multiplier of regression scores, clustered by RNA region and RBP (2,000 draws; seed 20261013). It is an **approximate exploratory interval**, not 2,000 full nuisance-model refits. This resource-bounded change from the local frozen plan is recorded before interpretation in the analysis manifest. We independently checked the same eight slopes with 200 actual full weighted least-squares refits (seed 20261014); all chr8/15 intervals spanned zero. A later exploratory sensitivity added NCBI region categories, including an explicit unavailable category, and performed another 200 weighted refits (seed 20261016). The hierarchy was coding, splice, UTR, noncoding exon, intronic, other annotated, unavailable; this annotation category is a nuisance grouping, not a measured SNV function. Full's region-adjusted slopes were 0.031 for directional credit (95% interval, −0.052 to +0.113) and 0.046 for ranked magnitude (−0.139 to +0.191). Paired Full−Sequence slope differences were also compatible with zero. No control, stratum or illustrative variant was selected for favorable model performance.

Center-PhyloP quartiles were determined from unique loci in each cohort, without using outcomes; tied scores stayed together. Unequal event and RBP coverage per quartile is retained in Data S25. The 7,262-event development-exposed analysis showed a small association between conservation and Full's predicted perturbation magnitude (adjusted standardized-rank slope 0.086; approximate interval 0.044–0.130), while the flat-Φ control also showed an association (0.045; 0.004–0.087). This descriptive development-set result does not imply a conservation-dependent improvement in correct natural-variant ordering and did not reproduce as a stable chr8/15 association.

### Public annotations and missingness

Before obtaining new annotations, we froze all 287 unique chr8/15 REF/ALT variants; no database-coverage or model-performance filtering altered the 396-event evaluation. We first requested official Ensembl VEP GRCh38 annotations (software release 116; REST release 15.12), then used the official NCBI Variation SPDI/RefSNP services after repeated HTTP500 failures. The SPDI query uses the GRCh38 reference accessions NC_000008.11 and NC_000015.10 and zero-based positions. Annotation requires an exact REF/ALT SPDI in the primary GRCh38 chromosome placement. For each population observation, equivalence to that specific allele is checked against retained RefSNP placements; merely sharing an rs identifier is insufficient.

NCBI returned exact allele matches for 279/287 variants; the remaining eight queries returned HTTP404 in two bounded attempts and remain unavailable. The primary original 1000 Genomes frequency table contains ALT allele counts and total chromosome counts for 241 variants, corresponding to 316 ASB events. We report ALT allele frequency as allele count/total count, retaining the study version, numerator and denominator. Of these, 239 study observations had two nonzero alleles and two had three; ALT AF should not be mistaken for MAF at multiallelic sites. Missing frequency is not AF=0. Ensembl succeeded for 110 variants and failed for 177; 101 variants had 1000 Genomes ALT AF from both services. Their maximal absolute difference was 4.89×10⁻⁵, consistent with Ensembl's rounded frequencies. Exact NCBI counts were retained as the primary source rather than choosing whichever annotation favored an association. Forty-four frequency observations from other studies lacked a directly retained equivalent placement and were excluded only from those source-specific annotation records, without excluding any ASB event or any primary 1000 Genomes frequency.

Thirty-one variants had allele-specific ClinVar links. Direct ClinVar esummary retrieval matched all 31 records by canonical SPDI and retained VCV version, current classification, review status and evaluation date. Current classifications were Benign (23), Benign/Likely benign (6), Likely benign (1) and Conflicting classifications of pathogenicity (1); older RefSNP RCV terms were preserved separately. Lack of a linked record is not proof that a current ClinVar entry does not exist. Clinical classifications and population frequency are provenance/context annotations, not measured RBP-binding effects or ground truth for this endpoint.

We inspected official GDC documentation for TCGA somatic variant data. Its tumor DNA mutation catalogs do not provide the matched allele-specific RBP-binding preference required for this experiment. Adding unlabelled TCGA mutations would change the scientific endpoint; therefore they were not mixed into the ASB evaluation. No controlled individual-level data were requested.

### Illustrative natural variant

The displayed SNV was selected by maximal distinct-RBP coverage across the complete chr8/15 cohort, breaking ties by greater center PhyloP, then chromosome, coordinate and allele. This uses coverage/reference information, not model advantage, and selects chr8:1,844,440 C>T in the ARHGEF10/KBTBD11-OT1 interval. All ten K562 RBP events are shown in alphabetical order. The source prefers C in all ten; Full predicts this direction in four and Sequence in six. Its center Φ is −3.501, so the figure does not imply that this site is strongly conserved. This single exposed example illustrates the mapping from a real SNV and fixed reference input to an observed preference and model response; it does not establish general biological causality.
